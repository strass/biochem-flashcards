Q: Ligand-receptor interaction
A: Specificity: molecular complementarity between ligand and receptor & in what cells have receptors. Sensitivity: affinity of receptors for their ligands, Kd can be <10e-7 M, meaning it has micromolar or nanomolar detection capability.

Q: Amplification of signaling cascade
A: Amplificaction results when an enzyme is activated by a signal receptor which in turn catalyzes the activation of many molecules of a second enzyme which will activate more again causing an exponential increase in the end product.

Q: Protein kinases and phosphoprotein phosphatases
A: A protein kinase uses ATP to put a phosphate group on a Ser, Thr, Tyr

Phosphatase removes phosphates, releasing the inorganic phosphate (Pi)

Commonly used in signal propagation as phosphorylation serves as an activating or deactivating signal.

Q: GPCRs
A: External ligand binding to receptor activates an intracellular GTP-binding protein which regulates an enzyme that generates an intracellular second messenger.

GPCRs have 7 transmembrane helices. Many types, each specific to a ligand but have common features. 

First messenger: Ligand binding outside the cell activates a G protein inside the cell. GDP, alpha, beta, gamma dissociate from the transmembrane bits and can activate other proteins such as adenlyl cyclase.

Second messenger: intermediary small molecule or ion that further transmits the signal (cAMP, DAG, Ca2+, IP3).

As a result, target molecules undergo chemical changes.

Q: Beta-adrenergic pathway
A: Epinephrine binds to its specific receptor. Allosteric change in hormone-receptor complex causes the GDP bound to Gsa to be replaced by GTP, activating Gsa.

Activated Gsa separates from Gsby, moves to adenylyl cyclase, and activates it. Many Gsa subunits may be activated by one occupied receptor.

Adenylyl cyclase catalyzes the synthesis of cAMP from ATP when associated with active Gsa. (second messenger)

cAMP activates PKA, which will phosphorylate cellular proteins, causing the response to epinephrine (eg Ca++ influx in heart or glucose availability in skeletal muscle)

Q: RTKs
A: Ligand binding activates tyrosine kinase activity by double autophosphorylation between dimers.

Family of plasma membrane receptors with protein kinase activity. Extracellular ligand binding domain and a cytoplasmic tyrosine kinase domaine.

Starts with inactive RTKs; signal molecule dimerizes and bonds to RTK, causing it to dimerize as well. Kinase activity is stimulated through autophosphorylation. Signal is relayed by activated signaling proteins into the cell's interior.

Q: insulin pathway
A: RTK example. Insulin binding and receptor dimerization; receptor autophosphorylation; IRS-1 phosphorylation (Tyr kinase) PI3K, AKT, AS160.

GLUT4 transporter translocates to the membrane and lets glucose in.

Q: EGFR receptor types
A: A few epidermal growth factor receptors. Can dimerize with many options. EGFR + HER2 + HER3 + HER4.

One type, HER2 dimer, is present in low amounts and does not require a ligand. Constantly signaling PI3K -> AKT. When this protein is amplified it can contribute to unregulated cell growth and survival (cancer).

Too much HER2 generates too many protein receptors, signaling for cancer cells to divide and multiply. Her2+ breast cancer. Herceptin (monoclonal antibody) works to block receptors and stop signal responsible for cancer cell growth and division.

Q: ion channel
A: Channel opens or closes in response to concentration of signal ligand or membrane potential

Ion channels underlie rapid electrical signaling in excitable cells (neurons). Ion channels open when a ligand binds or when voltage changes.

Na+K+ ATPase is electrogenic. Electrochemical potential determines spontaneous ion flow across a polarized membrane. When their respective channel opens Na+ and Ca2+ flow in, K+ and Cl- flow out. Na+K+ ATPase establishes that membrane potential, moving Na out and K in using ATP.

Opening and closing of ion channels underlies electrical signaling. Membrane potential at a given time depends on the types and numbers of ion channels open at any time.

Voltage gated ion channels produce neuronal action potentials. Action potential is a wave of depolarization (Na+ influx) followed by repolarization (K+ efflux). AP travels the length of a neuron carried by voltage gated Na+ and K+ channels of neuronal membranes. Arrival at distal end of a presynaptic neuron triggers neurotransmitter release.

Q: hormonal nuclear receptor
A: Hormone binding allows the receptor to regulate the expression of specific genes.

Steroid hormones enter directly into cell, bind to receptors, and regulate gene expression. Hormone response elements are specific regulatolry sequences in DNA that interact with receptor proteins following hormone binding. 

Hormone is carried to target tissue on serum binding proteins and diffuse across the plasma membrane and into the nucleus to bind to its receptor. Hormone binding changes the conformation of the recetpor, forming homo or heterodimers with other hormone-receptor complexes and binds to specific regulatory regions called hormone response elements (HREs) in the DNA adjacent to specific genes. Receptor attracts coactivator or corepressor proteins and regulates transcription of adjacent genes. Altered levels of hormone regulated gene product produce the cellular response to the hormone.
