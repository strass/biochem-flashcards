Q: How does PCR work?
A: Technique to amplify specific segments of DNA. Often need to generate large amounts of a DNA region of interest.

Reagents:
- Template DNA: unamplified DNA containing region of interest
- Primers: Mix of 2 short, single stranded DNA sequences that bind at the ends of the region to be amplified
- Taq DNA Polymerase: A DNA polymerase isolated from thermophilic bacteria and thus stable at high temperatures
- dNTPs: A mix of ATP, GTP, CTP, TTP (nucleotide triphosphates) that are used to extend DNA
- Mg2+: A co-factor required for DNA Polymerase to function
- Buffer: A buffer containing various ions at concentrations optimized for Taq DNA Polymerase activity

After combining reaction components, they are placed in thermocycler and a program is executed. Each cycle denatures (94C), primer annealing (55-60C), and extension (72C) and will be repeated 30 times with a initial denaturation & final extension step.

Taq DNA Polymerase reads 3'->5' and extends 5'-3'.

Theoretically creates 2^n copies but at high concentrations of amplified DNA this breaks down as the complementary amplified DNA will reform without primers.

Q: Gel electrophoresis
A: Can assess if DNA amplification occured and if products are the expected size.

Macromolecules like DNA, RNA, protein are loaded into a gel and exposed to an electric field. Since DNA is negatively charged it will move torwards the positive pole. Smaller molecules move more quickly than large ones. A ladder is added as a known reference to help reference size. Ethydium bromide is added and can visualize the DNA using UV light.

Q: 2d gel electrophoresis
A: A technique that combines an initial gel electrophoresis sorting molecules by charge with a second sorting them by size.

First a tubular gel with a pH gradient is loaded with a mix of proteins across the gel and an electric field is applied. Since the pH varies along the length of the gel the charge will carry each protein towards its isoelectric point. However this is not enough to identify proteins because multiple proteins can share an isoelectric point.

Next the isoelectric focusing gel is loaded onto a SDS-PAGE gel and an electric field is applied. SDS denatures the proteins and standardizes their electric charge; PAGE refers to a polyacrilimide gel. Proteins arranged laterally then sort themselves by size, moving towards the positive electrode.

Finally a dye (coomasie blue) is added and the proteins in the gel can be identified by their position on the 2D gel. For exact identification the section of gel can be cut out and run through something like NMR.

Q: Size exclusion chromatography
A: Separates proteins based on size by running through porous beads. Large proteins move around the beads completely while small ones travel more slowly through the pores. Size of largest pores sets exclusion limit of column. 

Fractions are eluted and will have proteins sorted by size.

Q: Affinity chromatography
A: Separates proteins by their ability to bind to specific molecules. Column is full of beads attached to the proteins ligand. As sample passes over column the proteins that bind the ligand get stuck while the rest pass by. Finally you wash the column with free ligand it will release protein from beads and can be collected.
