Q: Genomics
A: Entire DNA content of an organism - used to compare populations or to a "standard". NGS rapidly determines the sequence of a whole genome or just the exome. Manhattan plot shows SNPs, CRISPR can be used to edit genes.

Q: Epigenomics
A: Analysis of DNA and histone modifications that affect gene expression. Acetylation on histones helps determine whether the DNA is accessible for transcription to mRNA. Methylation of pyrimidines can silence a gene. If chemical changes occur in germ cells there is a chance that these differences in gene expression could be inherited.

Histone acetylation loosens DNA packing and makes it easier to transcribe.

Q: Transcriptomics
A: Analyzes all RNA transcripts (mRNAs) in a cell or tissue.

- Lets you compare two tissue types to determine which genes are expressed in each. 
- Comparing the same cell or tissue type before and after a treatment.
- Single cell RNA-seq on a circulating cancer cell to determine what genes are expressed that might be responsible for survival or could be drug targets.

Microarray: Isolate mRNA, reverse transcription -> cDNA (more stable) & fluorescent tagging. Hybridization onto microarray -> depending on fluorescent response you'll see which cells express which sequences.

Q: Proteomics & Metabolomics
A: Proteomics analyzes all the proteins in a cell or tissue and can encompass post-translational modifications. Similar to transcriptomics but takes into account that protein does not always follow mRNA. Proteins analyzed by mass spec which measures mass:charge ratio.

Metabolomics is similar but analyzes all small molecules in a sample which can include sugars and lipids. Often end products of metabolic processes. Useful for discovering biomarkers.

Q: Metagenomics
A: Analyzes all DNA in an environment. Useful for identifying unknown pathogens. Starting with an environmental sample all nucleic acids are extracted and sequenced. Obvious hits (liek human host DNA) are excluded.

Sequencing resuslt are compared against databases of all known microorganisms in hopes of finding a match or partial match. Helps detect emerging pathogens so early public health measures can be taken for prevention.
