Q: What is a competitive inhibitor?
A: An inhibitor that competes directly with the substrate to bind to the active site.

Q: What is a noncompetitive inhibitor?
A: An inhibitor that binds to a site other than the active site, blocking catalysis.

Q: What is a uncompetitive inhibitor?
A: An inhibitor that prevents catalysis by binding to the enzyme-substrate complex.

Q: How does a competitive inhibitor affect the V vs [S] plot?
A: Vmax unchanged, but higher [S] required to reach Vmax

Q: How does the Km of the reaction change in the presence of a competitive inhibitor?
A: It increases

Q: How does a noncompetitive inhibitor affect the V vs [S] plot?
A: Vmax smaller because fewer active enzymes. Km unchanged.

Q: How does a uncompetitive inhibitor affect the V vs [S] plot?
A: Vmax is smaller because enzymes blocked. There's actually more inhibition at higher [S]. Inhibitor depletes ES by forming ESI, shifting equilibrium towards ES (LeChatelier's principle). Enzyme is saturated at a lower substrate concentration, this decreases Km.
